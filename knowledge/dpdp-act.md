# India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (and DPDP Rules, 2025)

## Key facts
- The DPDP Act was enacted by Parliament on 11 August 2023 — India's first comprehensive personal data protection law.
- The DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 14 November 2025, operationalising the Act after 6,915 public consultation inputs.
- Compliance is phased over 18 months: Board provisions from 14 Nov 2025, consent-manager provisions from 14 Nov 2026, remaining substantive obligations from 14 May 2027.
- As of August 2026, the Data Protection Board of India is established (4 members, digital-first) but core obligations are still phasing in.
- The highest penalty — up to Rs 250 crore — applies to a Data Fiduciary's failure to maintain reasonable security safeguards.
- Failing to notify a data breach, or violating children's data obligations, can each draw penalties up to Rs 200 crore.
- Any other violation of the Act or Rules by a Data Fiduciary can attract penalties up to Rs 50 crore.
- A "Data Fiduciary" decides why and how personal data is processed; a "Data Principal" is the individual the data is about.
- Consent must be clear, informed, specific and easy to understand — and withdrawable at any time.
- Every Data Fiduciary must issue a separate, plain-language consent notice stating the specific purpose of data use.
- On a breach, affected individuals must be informed without delay; a detailed report goes to the Board within 72 hours (Rules).
- Data Principals can access, correct, update and erase their data; fiduciaries must respond within 90 days.
- Children's data needs verifiable consent from a parent or guardian; tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads at children are prohibited.
- Appeals against Data Protection Board decisions go to the TDSAT (Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal).
- Consent Managers — one-stop platforms to give, review and withdraw consent — must be companies incorporated in India.

## The law rests on seven principles
- Consent and transparency.
- Purpose limitation.
- Data minimisation.
- Accuracy.
- Storage limitation.
- Security safeguards.
- Accountability.

## Data fiduciary duties
- Process personal data only for lawful purposes, with consent or for certain "legitimate uses".
- Give clear, itemised notice of what data is collected and why.
- Maintain reasonable security safeguards to prevent breaches (highest penalty tier if not).
- Notify the Board and affected Data Principals of personal data breaches.
- Erase data when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is served.
- Display a contact point (e.g. Data Protection Officer) for data-related queries.
- Significant Data Fiduciaries additionally need independent audits, impact assessments and, in some cases, data localisation for restricted categories.

## Data principal rights
- Right to give or refuse consent, and to withdraw it at any time.
- Right to know what data is collected, why, and how it is used.
- Right to access a copy of one's personal data.
- Right to correction, updating and erasure.
- Right to nominate another person to exercise these rights.
- Right to grievance redressal — complaints can be filed online with the Data Protection Board.

## Status note (verified August 2026)
- Rules notified 14 Nov 2025; Data Protection Board established in the National Capital Region with four members.
- Consent-manager registration and related provisions take effect 14 Nov 2026.
- Notices, breach reporting, children's data rules, rights procedures and penalties become fully enforceable by 14 May 2027.
