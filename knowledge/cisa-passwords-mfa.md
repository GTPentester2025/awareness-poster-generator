# Strong Passwords, Password Managers and MFA (CISA)

## Key facts
- Enabling MFA makes you 99% less likely to be hacked, according to CISA.
- The most common password in the US is still "123456" (CISA).
- CISA's password rule of three: make passwords LONG (at least 16 characters), RANDOM, and UNIQUE per account.
- A memorable alternative to random strings: a passphrase of 4-7 unrelated words, e.g. "Horse Purple Hat Run Bay Lifting" (CISA).
- Reused passwords are a chain reaction: one breached account can unlock your email, bank and socials.
- CISA recommends a password manager to generate, store and autofill strong unique passwords.
- With a password manager you only memorize one strong password — the manager's own master passphrase.
- Password managers also flag weak or reused passwords so you can fix them (CISA).
- MFA means using more than a password to log in: a code, an authenticator app, a security key, or a biometric.
- Even if criminals steal your password, MFA blocks them at the second step (CISA).
- Turn on MFA for every account that offers it — prioritize email, banking, and social media (CISA).
- Not all MFA is equal: CISA calls FIDO/WebAuthn security keys and passkeys "the gold standard" of MFA.
- SMS and voice-call MFA are the weakest forms — vulnerable to SIM-swap and SS7 attacks — but still far better than no MFA (CISA).
- Authenticator apps generate a fresh code every 30 seconds and beat SMS codes for security (CISA).

## Building a strong password (CISA "Secure Our World")
- Long: 16+ characters — longer is stronger.
- Random: a mixed string like "cXmnZK65rf*&DaaD", or a multi-word passphrase.
- Unique: a different password for every single account.
- Never store passwords in a plain file — use a password manager instead.
- If a site allows short or dictionary passwords for your key accounts, ask them why.

## Turning on MFA (three easy steps)
- Step 1: open the account's Settings (Account, Profile, Privacy or similar).
- Step 2: find Security settings and turn on MFA (may be called two-factor or two-step authentication).
- Step 3: pick your method — texted/emailed code, authenticator app, or biometrics — and confirm.
- Best choice where offered: a phishing-resistant method such as a FIDO2 security key or passkey.

## MFA strength ladder (weakest to strongest, per CISA)
- SMS or voice-call codes — better than nothing, but phishable and SIM-swappable.
- App-generated one-time codes (authenticator apps) — good.
- App push notifications with number matching — better.
- Phishing-resistant FIDO/WebAuthn keys and passkeys — the gold standard.

## What MFA protects (CISA)
- Banking and financial accounts.
- Online purchases and payment apps.
- Email — the master key to your other accounts.
- Social media profiles.
- Business systems and your identity.

## Poster-ready reminders
- One password, one account — never recycle.
- 16 characters minimum: size matters for passwords.
- Let a password manager do the remembering.
- MFA takes a moment; account recovery takes weeks.
