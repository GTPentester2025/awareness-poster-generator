# EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

## Key facts
- GDPR is Regulation (EU) 2016/679; it has applied across the EU/EEA since 25 May 2018.
- It protects any information relating to an identified or identifiable person — "personal data" (Art. 4(1)).
- It applies even to companies outside the EU if they offer goods/services to, or monitor, people in the EU (Art. 3).
- Data breaches must be reported to the supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware (Art. 33).
- If a breach poses high risk to individuals, those affected must also be told without undue delay (Art. 34).
- Top-tier fines reach EUR 20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover — whichever is higher (Art. 83(5)).
- Lower-tier fines reach EUR 10 million or 2% of worldwide annual turnover (Art. 83(4)).
- Processing is only lawful with one of six legal bases, e.g. consent, contract, or legitimate interests (Art. 6).
- Consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous — and as easy to withdraw as to give (Art. 7).
- You have the right to see the personal data an organisation holds about you, free of charge (Art. 15).
- You can demand correction of wrong data (Art. 16) and deletion — the "right to be forgotten" (Art. 17).
- You can take your data with you to another provider in a machine-readable format (Art. 20).
- You can object to processing, including direct marketing, which must then stop (Art. 21).
- You have the right not to be subject to purely automated decisions with legal or similar significant effects (Art. 22).
- Some organisations must appoint a Data Protection Officer, e.g. public bodies and large-scale monitors (Art. 37).

## Core principles (Art. 5)
- Lawfulness, fairness and transparency — process data legally and openly.
- Purpose limitation — collect data only for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes.
- Data minimisation — collect only what is adequate, relevant and necessary.
- Accuracy — keep data accurate and up to date.
- Storage limitation — keep data no longer than needed.
- Integrity and confidentiality — keep data secure (security principle).
- Accountability — the controller must be able to demonstrate compliance.

## Data subject rights (Art. 15-22)
- Art. 15 — right of access to your data and information about its use.
- Art. 16 — right to rectification of inaccurate data.
- Art. 17 — right to erasure ("right to be forgotten").
- Art. 18 — right to restriction of processing.
- Art. 19 — controllers must notify recipients of rectification/erasure/restriction.
- Art. 20 — right to data portability.
- Art. 21 — right to object (absolute for direct marketing).
- Art. 22 — rights around automated decision-making and profiling.

## Lawful bases for processing (Art. 6)
- Consent of the data subject.
- Performance of a contract.
- Compliance with a legal obligation.
- Protection of vital interests (life or death situations).
- Performance of a task in the public interest / official authority.
- Legitimate interests of the controller (balanced against individual rights).

## DPO requirements (Art. 37-39)
- Mandatory for public authorities and bodies (except courts acting judicially).
- Mandatory where core activities involve large-scale, regular and systematic monitoring of individuals.
- Mandatory where core activities involve large-scale processing of special categories or criminal data (Art. 9-10).
- The DPO reports to top management and must be independent and adequately resourced (Art. 38).
