# Personal Data Hygiene Basics

## Key facts
- Personal data is any information relating to an identifiable person: name, ID numbers, location data, online identifiers, and more (GDPR Art. 4(1)).
- Seemingly harmless details combine: birthday + hometown + pet's name can crack security questions and passwords.
- Revealing too much on social media gives criminals material to phish you specifically (CISA).
- IC3 received 67,456 personal-data-breach complaints in 2025 — one of the top four reported cybercrimes.
- If your identity is stolen, IdentityTheft.gov gives a step-by-step recovery plan (FTC).
- Check app permissions: only grant access (location, camera, mic, contacts) that the app truly needs (FTC).
- Some apps track precise location even when not in use — review and restrict location permissions in settings (FTC).
- Delete apps you no longer use; fewer apps means less data collection and a smaller attack surface (FTC).
- Most websites now use HTTPS encryption, which has made public Wi-Fi far safer than it once was (FTC).
- On public Wi-Fi, still avoid sites without HTTPS, and don't let devices auto-connect to unknown networks.
- Social engineering exploits human trust — attackers pose as IT support, banks, or colleagues to extract data (CISA).
- Be suspicious of unsolicited calls or emails asking about employees, systems, or personal details (CISA).
- Never reveal personal or financial information by email, and don't respond to solicitations for it (CISA).
- Lock down social media profiles: strangers don't need your travel plans, workplace, or kids' school.
- Data you post is forever: assume anything shared online can be copied, scraped, and never fully deleted.

## What counts as personal data
- Direct identifiers: name, photo, phone number, email address, government ID numbers.
- Indirect identifiers: device IDs, IP addresses, cookies, location traces, browsing history.
- Sensitive categories: health, biometrics, finances, religion, sexual orientation — deserving extra care.
- Combinations matter: three or four "anonymous" data points can often re-identify a specific person.

## Everyday habits that protect you
- Think before you post: would you hand this info to a stranger on the street?
- Review app permissions on install and again every few months.
- Turn off location sharing for apps that don't need it; post vacation photos after you're home.
- Use unique passwords plus MFA so a leak of one account can't cascade.
- Prefer HTTPS sites; consider a VPN on untrusted networks for extra protection.
- Shred documents and wipe old devices before disposal — data lives on hardware too.
- Verify any request for personal data directly with the organization using a known channel (CISA).

## Public Wi-Fi in practice
- Look for the padlock/HTTPS on every page where you enter data, not just the login page.
- Turn off automatic connection to open Wi-Fi networks in your device settings.
- Beware lookalike hotspot names ("Airport_Free_WiFi" vs the venue's real network).
- When in doubt, use your phone's mobile data or hotspot for banking and shopping.

## Common oversharing traps
- Quiz posts ("your first pet + street name") harvest password-reset answers.
- Boarding pass and ticket photos expose barcodes with personal booking data.
- Real-time vacation posts advertise an empty home.
- Public birthday and family details enable convincing impersonation scams.
- "Check-in" posts build a searchable timeline of where you are and when.
- Old, forgotten accounts still hold your data — close what you don't use.
