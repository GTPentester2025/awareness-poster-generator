# Recognizing and Reporting Phishing (CISA / FTC / FBI IC3)

## Key facts
- Phishing/spoofing was the #1 reported cybercrime to the FBI's IC3 in 2025 with 191,561 complaints.
- Reported phishing/spoofing losses hit $215.8 million in 2025 (FBI IC3 2025 Annual Report).
- Overall, IC3 logged a record 1,008,597 complaints and $20.877 billion in losses in 2025 — up 26% from 2024.
- The average reported loss per IC3 complaint in 2025 was $20,699.
- People aged 60+ were hit hardest: 201,266 complaints and $7.7 billion in losses in 2025.
- CISA red flag: urgent or emotionally charged language threatening dire consequences if you don't act now.
- CISA red flag: requests for personal or financial information out of the blue.
- CISA red flag: untrusted shortened URLs, or addresses/links that are slightly wrong (e.g. "amazn.com").
- Don't rely on bad grammar to spot scams — CISA warns AI now gives phishing emails perfect spelling.
- FTC warning signs: generic greetings, fake invoices, "suspicious activity" alerts, and links to "update payment info".
- CISA's three steps: Recognize the signs, Resist clicking links or attachments, Delete the message.
- Never click "unsubscribe" in a suspicious message — CISA says just delete it.
- Verify independently: contact the company via its real website or a number you already have, never via the message.
- Legitimate companies will not email or text you a link to update your payment information (FTC).

## Common phishing lures (FTC)
- "We've noticed suspicious activity or log-in attempts" — they haven't.
- "There's a problem with your account or payment information" — there isn't.
- "You must confirm some personal or financial information" — you don't.
- An invoice you don't recognize — it's fake.
- A link to click to make a payment — the link has malware.
- "You're eligible to register for a government refund" — it's a scam.
- A coupon for free stuff — it's not real.

## What to do if you took the bait
- If you shared personal or financial data, go to IdentityTheft.gov for a tailored recovery plan (FTC).
- If you clicked a link or opened an attachment, update your security software and run a full scan (FTC).
- Change the affected password immediately — and everywhere else you reused it — then turn on MFA.
- Watch bank and card statements for unauthorized charges and alert your bank.

## How to report phishing
- Forward phishing emails to CISA at report@cisa.gov.
- Report cyber incidents to CISA online at cisa.gov/report or call (888) 282-0870.
- Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) — works on major carriers.
- Forward phishing emails to the Anti-Phishing Working Group at reportphishing@apwg.org (FTC guidance).
- Report fraud to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
- Report internet crime and losses to the FBI at ic3.gov — IC3 now averages nearly 3,000 complaints per day.
- Use the "report spam/phishing" button in your mail client so filters improve for everyone (CISA).

## Poster-ready reminders
- Think before you click: urgency is the scammer's favorite weapon.
- If a message looks suspicious, it probably is — report it, then delete it.
- No real bank, boss, or brand needs your password by email.
- Slow down: hover over links and check the real address before you click.
- Perfect grammar no longer means legit — AI writes flawless scams now.
- Got phished? Report it — your report protects the next target.
